Privacy Policy

Last updated 20 November 2026

Nordram reads screenshots of your bank app so you don’t have to type your spending in by hand. That means we handle information about your money, so here is exactly what we do with it — in plain language, with nothing important left out.

Nordram is a small independent project based in Armenia.

The short version

  • We are not connected to your bank. We only ever see what you choose to show us.
  • We never ask for your email address or phone number.
  • We do not keep your screenshots.
  • We do not sell your data, and there are no adverts in Nordram.

What we keep, and what we never keep

We keep

  • The payments you import or add — the amount, currency, date, place and category.
  • Your username, your currency, and the rules you set for budgets, savings, trips and accounts.
  • If you create an account, your password, stored scrambled. We cannot read it.
  • A record of each import: when it happened, and a fingerprint of each picture, so the same screenshot is not imported twice by accident.

We never keep

  • Your screenshots. They are read, then gone.
  • Your card number, PIN or bank login. We never ask for them.
  • Your email address or phone number.
  • Your location, your contacts, or anything else on your phone.

You start as a guest, signed in with no username and no password. If you create an account, Nordram signs you in with a username and a password, and nothing else. So that the sign-in system has an address to work with, one is generated from your username behind the scenes. It is not a mailbox, nothing is ever sent to it, and it is never shown to you or to anyone else.

What happens to a screenshot, a statement or a receipt

When you import a screenshot of your bank app, a statement (a PDF or a CSV file) or a photo of a receipt, it is sent to an AI service to be read. It sends back the amounts, dates and places it found. We keep those numbers. The file itself is not stored.

A picture or a PDF is sent as it is. A CSV statement is sent as its text. In one import a picture can go to the service more than once: it can first be checked to tell a receipt from a bank screen, and a receipt’s picture is sent again so that a category can be suggested from the items on it. Each time it goes to the same service, with the same settings.

The AI service is Google’s Gemini, reached through a provider called OpenRouter. Every file is sent with settings that instruct the provider not to retain it and not to use it to train models. A PDF is read by Gemini itself, not handed to a separate document reader. The reading happens on Google’s global infrastructure, which means a file may be processed on servers outside Armenia and outside Europe.

Where your data is kept

Your account and your payment records are stored with Supabase, on servers in Frankfurt, Germany.

Who can see it

Nordram is small, and the person who builds it can see the transactions you import. Nobody reads them as a habit — but nothing technical prevents it, and we would rather tell you that than claim otherwise. When that changes, so does this line.

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with anyone else apart from the services named on this page — today that is the AI service that reads your screenshots, the company that stores your account, and the service that measures how the app is used. If we ever add another, we will name it here before it starts.

Measuring how the app is used

This is switched on. The app records which screens people open and which steps fail, so we can fix what is broken. These records go to a service called PostHog, on its servers in the European Union.

What it records: the app opening; the screens you open; an import starting, finishing, failing or being undone; which detail you corrected, such as the date or the category, but never what you changed it to; opening the charts; money moved between your own accounts, but never how much; which part of the place list a place came from, but never the place; what kind of files you shared into the app, and how many; and, while you are a guest, being asked to save your account, saving it (with how many days and how many payments you had by then), reaching the picture limit, or a guest start that failed.

Each record carries a random number made on your phone — not your username and not your account — plus the app version and your phone’s system and its version. Against that number the app also sends how many days since you started, how many currencies you hold (one, two, or three or more), how many imports you have made, and whether you are a guest. It never carries an amount, a shop, a bank or a file name. The app does not record your taps on its own, and it does not record your screen. We ask PostHog not to work out where you are from your internet address. There is no setting in the app to turn this off. Because these records are not tied to your account, deleting your account does not delete them.

Nordram also has crash reporting built in, so that when the app breaks we can find out why. That is not switched on yet — the app is not connected to a crash reporting service and no report is being sent anywhere. When it is connected, this is what will happen. Before a report leaves your phone we remove the things a computer can recognise reliably: numbers, and email addresses. Free text is harder — if the name of a shop was part of what went wrong, it can travel with the report. So we will not tell you these reports are anonymous. They carry no username, and they are not tied to your account.

Deleting your data

You can delete your account yourself, from Profile inside the app, whenever you want, whether you are still a guest or have created an account. It removes the account itself and not only what is in it: every payment you imported or logged, your categories, budgets and trips. If you created an account, the username and password you sign in with go too, and the username is free for someone else straight away. Every device you are signed in on is signed out.

A guest has no username or password to sign back in with, so signing out of a guest account erases it in the same way. The app asks you to confirm first, and offers to create an account instead.

We keep no backup, so this cannot be undone and there is nothing left for us to restore. If you would rather we did it for you, write to the address below.

Children

Nordram is not intended for anyone under 16.
